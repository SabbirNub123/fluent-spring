Part A: Skimming

Definition

Reading a text quickly to understand its general idea or main message.

Purpose

To decide what a text is about and whether it is useful, without reading every word.

When to use it

  • Choosing which article to read for an assignment
  • Previewing a chapter before a lecture
  • Checking what a news report or email is about

How to skim

  1. Read the title.
  2. Read the introduction.
  3. Read the headings.
  4. Read the first sentence of each paragraph.
  5. Read the conclusion.

Why Group Study Helps University Students

Many students prefer to study alone, but many teachers believe that studying with classmates has real advantages. This article explains three of them.

1. Sharing ideas

When students discuss a topic, they hear explanations they would not think of alone. This helps them notice details they may have missed while reading.

2. Staying motivated

Group study makes it easier to keep a regular study routine. Friends remind each other about deadlines and encourage each other when a subject feels difficult.

3. Learning by explaining

Explaining a difficult idea to a classmate is a powerful way to test your own understanding. If you cannot explain it clearly, you know what to review.

In conclusion, group study is not a replacement for individual reading, but it is a useful addition that can improve understanding and motivation.

Part B: Scanning

Definition

Looking through a text quickly to locate specific information such as names, dates, numbers, places or keywords.

Purpose

To find one exact answer fast, without reading the rest of the text.

When to use it

  • Finding an exam date on a notice
  • Looking for a phone number or room number
  • Checking a name or a statistic in an article

How to scan

  1. Decide exactly what you need (a date? a name?).
  2. Think about how it looks: digits, capital letters, month names.
  3. Move your eyes quickly over the text.
  4. When you find it, read the words around it to confirm.
  5. Stop. Do not read the rest.

Practical example: search for specific information

Choose a type of information to highlight. Names and places often start with capital letters. Dates and numbers contain digits.
